# Glossary

Terms used across the workbooks and manifest before they're ever defined in place. Read this once in Week 1; come back anytime a term doesn't ring a bell.

- **ICP** — Ideal Customer Profile. Here, the specific kind of investor you're targeting (not a product customer). Lives in `icp.md`.
- **GTM** — Go-to-Market. How you get your product (or your fundraise) in front of the people who'd want it.
- **DPI** — Distributions to Paid-In capital. A measure of how much cash a VC fund has actually returned to its own investors. High DPI pressure means a fund needs exits soon, which changes what they'll say yes to.
- **ESOP** — Employee Stock Option Pool. Shares set aside for the team, factored into your dilution math in `raise.md`.
- **Pigment** (Pigment.is) — the strengths-assessment tool every founder completes in Week 1. Tells you what kind of work drains you, which is what Week 2's agent roster gets built around.
- **breadboard** — this repo. Named for a breadboard in electronics: where you prototype a circuit before committing it to a permanent board. You clone or copy from it, then replace every template with your own live files by Week 4.
- **canon.md** — your single source of truth: your story plus every number you claim, each with a source. Everything else (agents, deck, data room) reads from this file.
- **manifest** — the set of six strategy files (`canon.md`, `raise.md`, `icp.md`, `pipeline.md`, `goals.md`, `committee.md`) that make up your company's strategy, as files. (`pipeline.md` is the plain-text mirror of the Notion CRM-of-record; the other five are file-first.)

## Words this programme invented

These look like ordinary English, which is exactly why they're easy to misread. They mean something specific here.

**Week 1**

- **skill file** — a plain-text file that tells an AI model what job to do. You paste it in as instructions; it never changes. The files in `skills/` are all skill files.
- **voice spec** — the 8–12 rules describing how you actually write: sentence length, words you use and never use, punctuation habits. Derived once in Week 1 from samples of your own writing, then every agent reads it so their drafts sound like you. Lives in `canon.md § Voice spec`.
- **voice sample** — the raw, unpolished writing the voice spec is derived *from*. Different thing, different section.
- **objection ledger** — the five hardest questions investors ask you, each with your best current answer. What you say *during* a conversation. The AI session hands you the entries; you don't have to pick them. Lives in `canon.md § Objection ledger`. One question-and-answer pair is a **ledger entry**.
- **OPEN** — what you write instead of an answer you don't have yet, plus what it would take to close it. Used in the ledger and against any number you can't source. Writing OPEN is the correct move, not a failure.
- **kill question** — the single question you handled worst. You don't judge this yourself: `skills/objections.md` names it at the end of every session. Pinned at the top of your ledger; you re-run until it isn't.
- **follow-up draft** — a half-written message you send *after* an investor conversation, with a deliberate gap for the thing you only learn on the day. Not a template: templates are finished, these are not. Three of them, written once in Week 1 and reused all month. Different from the objection ledger, which is what you say *during* the conversation.
- **traction** — evidence that people actually want this: users, revenue, retention, signed contracts. Numbers, not intentions.
- **anti-goal** — something you are deliberately *not* doing this year, written down so nobody quietly does it anyway.
- **scoreable** — specific enough that an agent could tell from evidence alone whether you hit it, without asking you. The bar every goal in `goals.md` has to clear.
- **one-pager / investor brief** — the single page an investor actually screens: problem, market, edge, traction, the ask. Baseline collected in Week 1, assembled properly in Week 3.

**Week 2**

- **the panel** — five AI investor personas in `panel/` that score your narrative each week against `panel/rubric.md`. Not real investors; pressure-testing, never prediction.
- **rubric** — the scoring sheet the panel and your peers both use: three axes (Evidence, Voice, Specificity), scored 1–5. One rubric, all four weeks.
- **agent org / roster** — the list of agents you've built and what each one's job is, designed to cover the work your Pigment profile says drains you.
- **delta report** — what the auditor gives you: what you actually shipped this week versus what you said you'd ship. The gap is the point.
- **alpha / beta** — alpha (Week 2) is your agent team running internally, tested against the panel and committee only, no real investor touched. Beta (Week 4) is the same team running live against real investors.

**Week 3**

- **milestone chain** — the logic in `raise.md` linking each pound you raise to a milestone it buys and the proof that milestone produces. A round with no milestone chain is a budget, not a raise.
- **disqualifier** — a rule for who you will *not* approach. An ICP with no disqualifiers isn't an ICP; it's "investors".
- **thesis fit** — whether an investor already believes the thing you'd need to convince them of. Cheaper to find than to create.
- **committee seat** — one real person on your advisory committee, with quotes and a date attached. Starts as a generic persona from `panel/`, gets replaced by someone you actually met.
- **signal log** — one row per real investor interaction — reply, call, dinner, roundtable — with what stage it reached and what came back. The receipt that you got in front of real money.
- **cap table** — who owns what percentage of your company. **Dilution** is how much your share shrinks when you issue new shares. **Runway** is how many months of cash you have left at current burn.
