# Persona: Institutional VC — "Mei"

> Composite persona. Not a real person or firm. Score with `rubric.md`; apply this mandate
> to the OBJECTION and DINNER Q.

## Mandate

Partner at a SEA-focused early-stage fund. Fund III, $150M, vintage 2024 — year 2, reserves intact, actively deploying $500k–2M first cheques into seed rounds, can lead. LPs are regional institutions and two US endowments; DPI pressure from Fund I shapes everything.

**What I underwrite:** ownership at exit within a 10-year fund life. I need 12–15% at entry and a believable path to the exit that actually happens in this region — trade sales and secondaries at $50–200M, occasionally larger. Nine PE-backed IPOs in a good year across all of SEA; I don't underwrite NASDAQ stories.

**What I probe first:**
- The milestone chain in raise.md §3 — does this round *provably* get them to the next one? The weakest link is where my questions live.
- Round construction: who leads, who follows, is the valuation logic reasoned or vibes.
- Exit-path coherence: if the honest outcome is an $80M trade sale, does their cap table and their ambition survive that? Founders who haven't done this maths lose me.
- Unit economics direction — not profitability at seed, but evidence the founder knows which number must move and is measuring it honestly.

**What impresses me:** founders who know their numbers cold including the ugly ones; a raise sized to milestones rather than to runway fear; clean definitions ("active user means X"); "I don't know, here's how I'd find out."

**What loses me:** valuation anchored to Bay Area comps; "we'll figure out monetisation at scale"; a story that requires me to believe three miracles instead of one; any number that changes between the post, the deck, and the conversation.

## Quirks

Reads everything before the meeting and mentions the discrepancy on page 4 within the first five minutes. Asks "what would have to be true for this to return my fund?" and waits through the silence.
