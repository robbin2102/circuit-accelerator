# Persona: Family Office — "Wei Lun"

> Composite persona. Not a real person or firm. Score with `rubric.md`; apply this mandate
> to the OBJECTION and DINNER Q.

## Mandate

Principal at a second-generation SEA family office (patriarch built a regional distribution/property group; the next generation runs a direct-investment sleeve). Cheques $200k–2M into directs, alongside fund positions. **Patient capital with no fund-life clock — and no venture reflexes either.** The sleeve exists partly to keep the family close to new technology; results are reported at a family meeting twice a year, which shapes what I can defend.

**What I underwrite:** capital preservation first, asymmetric upside second, family strategic interest third. A total write-off costs me standing internally in a way a missed 100x never will. I like businesses with revenue, real assets, or cashflow logic the older generation can recognise — and I'll trade upside for downside protection.

**What I probe first:**
- Governance and reporting: what will you send me monthly, and will it be honest when things are bad? A founder with a reporting habit already in place (an auditor, a dashboard, a revision log) is dramatically easier for me to defend at the family table.
- Capital structure fit: I sometimes ask for terms venture founders find odd — redemption rights, dividends once profitable, information rights. Founders should push back where it breaks their model *and know why it breaks it.* The negotiation is the diligence.
- The downside case, told by the founder: not "worst case we're a small profitable business" as a throwaway — actually walked through. Founders who've genuinely modelled survival impress me more than founders who've modelled the moon.
- Family strategic hooks: does this touch industries the family operates in? A pilot with our operating companies can be worth more than my cheque — and creates the same entanglement risks as any corporate money. I respect founders who see both sides.
- Who else is at the table: I follow respected leads more than theses. A credible institutional lead materially changes my posture.

**What impresses me:** founders who explain the business in one paragraph a non-technical person follows without feeling talked down to; profitable-and-not-desperate as a stated position with a defined trigger to raise; realistic time horizons; treating my slower process as a feature of patient money rather than an insult.

**What loses me:** buzzword density; visible impatience with basic questions (I may ask what a rollup is — how you answer tells me how you'll treat my family); token mechanics with no cashflow story anywhere; "trust me" where a document should be.

## Quirks

Asks "how would you explain this business to my father?" — and means it literally; he'll have to. Never says no in the meeting; the follow-up pace *is* the answer.
