# Narrative Rubric

One rubric, four weeks, both lenses. The panel scores against it; peer pairs review against it; Kitty/Lani spot-check against it. Defined in Week 1 so improvement is measurable by Week 4.

**What gets scored:** the week's narrative artifact — Friday post draft, narrative one-pager, or pitch/podcast transcript — read alongside the team's manifest.

## The three axes (score each 1–5)

### A. Evidence — claim → evidence → stakes
Every claim carries its source; every number is in canon.md; the stakes of each claim are explicit (why it matters, not just that it happened).

- **1** — Claims float free. Numbers appear that aren't in canon.md, or contradict it.
- **3** — Key claims sourced, but at least one load-bearing number is vague ("thousands of users") or unverifiable, or stakes are left for the reader to infer.
- **5** — Every claim checkable against canon.md; measurement definitions clear; the "so what" of each number is stated. An investor could diligence this artifact from its own contents.

### B. Voice — sounds like the founder
- **1** — Interchangeable AI cadence. Any of the seven teams could have posted it. Contains "excited to announce."
- **3** — Recognisably human, but polished into anonymity; the founder's actual speech patterns surface only occasionally.
- **5** — Unmistakably this founder. A person who knows them would believe it was written on their phone in one sitting.

### C. Specificity — names, dates, amounts
- **1** — "Significant traction," "major partnership," "growing fast." Adjectives doing the work numbers should do.
- **3** — Some concrete detail, but the most important sentence is still abstract.
- **5** — Concrete throughout: named things, dated events, counted amounts, one memorable detail. Nothing a competitor could copy-paste about themselves.

## The pairwise verdict (most reliable output — weight it over the scores)

Compare against the same team's previous artifact: **STRONGER / WEAKER / FLAT — with the single biggest reason.** This is the primary week-over-week signal.

## Persona output format (every scoring session returns exactly this)

```
AXIS SCORES:  Evidence: n/5 · Voice: n/5 · Specificity: n/5
PAIRWISE:     STRONGER | WEAKER | FLAT vs last week — one-sentence reason
OBJECTION:    The single biggest problem from THIS persona's mandate (≤3 sentences)
DINNER Q:     The one question I would ask this founder at dinner
```

Personas apply their mandate lens to the OBJECTION and DINNER Q only. Axis scores follow this rubric identically for all five personas — mandate ≠ different scoring standards.

## Worked scoring examples (anchor against these; they are the drift check)

**Example 1 — scores Evidence 2, Voice 2, Specificity 2:**
> "Huge week for us! We're excited to announce a major partnership with a leading regional player. Our community is growing faster than ever and the feedback has been incredible. This is just the beginning of the journey 🚀"
*Why: no named partner (and "partnership" undefined — signed? in talks?), no numbers, "excited to announce," could be any startup on earth.*

**Example 2 — scores Evidence 4, Voice 4, Specificity 5:**
> "Tuesday, 2:14am: our reconciliation job flagged a mismatch — 41 transactions, $3,880, all from one integration partner. Took us 11 hours to find the bug (ours, not theirs). Fixed, refunded, and we now run the job hourly instead of daily. Painful week to write about, but this is what 'we handle money carefully' actually costs. 9,340 users as of Friday (dashboard in bio)."
*Why: dated, counted, sourced (public dashboard), stakes explicit, a memorable detail, and the willingness to publish a failure — Evidence 4 not 5 only because the partner is unnamed. Voice 4 assumes it matches the founder's spec — check.*

**Scoring discipline:** 5s must stay rare — a 5 means "would not improve with another hour of work." If your average this week is higher than last week for every team, suspect your own drift before believing the cohort improved uniformly.
